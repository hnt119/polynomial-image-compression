### The witness and bridge

Let $K=F_j$ be a totally real transfer layer of degree $d$, with root discriminant at most $D$. Select $t$ distinct rational primes splitting completely in every selected layer, fix their product $Q$, and put $T=Q/8$. The parameters are fixed before $d\to\infty$. Write $B(L)=\{b\in\mathcal O_K:|\sigma b|\le L\text{ for every real }\sigma\}$ and $A=\iota(B(T))/Q$ for one real embedding. A translate of a side-$T$ cube contains at least $T^d/\sqrt{|\Delta_K|}$ lattice points. Subtract any one of them to put that many distinct points in $B(T)$. Norm integrality separates distinct algebraic integers by at least one in sup norm. Thus
$$
 (Q/(8\sqrt D))^d\le N=|A|=|B(T)|\le (Q/4+1)^d.
$$
In particular $Q/8>\sqrt D$ suffices for growth. This inequality is a growth condition, independent of the injectivity argument at one layer. In the imported tower construction, the finite normal closure of the Frattini quotient has odd 3-power degree. After adjoining $i$, its unique quadratic subfield is $\mathbb Q(i)$, distinct from $\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)$. The Chebotarev prescriptions for identity on the former extension and either nonidentity or identity on the latter give the two prime classes. In the identity case, complete splitting in their compositum suffices. The fixed splitting constraints then enter the supplied unramified tower construction. [First checkpoint §§A.1–A.2, lines 40–167](https://github.com/hnt119/polynomial-image-compression/blob/bd1f2d663262821575eebdfa559b7c25f76b46ab/research/history-sources/quadratic_to_cubic_transfer_checkpoint.md#L40-L167)

Set $P_2=(x-y)^2+5(x+y)$ and $P_-=(x-y)^3-3(x+y)$. With $u=x-y$, direct expansion gives $5P_-=5u^3+3u^2-3P_2$. Knowing both $u$ and $P_2$ recovers $x+y=(P_2-u^2)/5$ and both coordinates. The report’s sample $(1,0),(0,1)$ gives the common quadratic value 6 and cubic values $-2,-4$. For integral numerators $a,b$, put $v=a-b$ and $s=a+b$. The cleared outputs are $S=v^2+5Qs$ and $Z=v^3-3Q^2s$. The embedding and real rescalings preserve equality and cardinality.

Modulo $Q$, the quadratic output lies in at most $(Q\rho)^d$ classes, where $\rho=\prod_{q\mid Q}(q+1)/(2q)$. In each class the archimedean output interval has length at most $4T^2+20QT$, and distinct points are $Q$-separated in sup norm. Comparing the support bound with the squared input lower bound gives $|P_2(A,A)|/N^2\le\Theta^d$, with $\Theta=D\rho(4+20Q/T+Q/T^2)=D\rho(164+64/Q)$. If $\Theta<1$, a fixed quadratic saving follows from the input upper bound. For the first checkpoint’s chosen 5-mod-12 primes, excluding 5 gives $Q\ge17^t$ and $\rho\le(9/17)^t$. The supplied bounds $t=\lfloor(\ell-1)^2/100\rfloor$ and $\log D=O(\ell\log\ell)$ allow one sufficiently large fixed $\ell$ to give both $Q/(8\sqrt D)>1$ and $\Theta<1$. From the input upper bound, the certified quadratic exponent can be taken as $\min\{1,-\log\Theta/\log(Q/4+1)\}$. The transfer results test these prescribed benchmark inputs against the full cubic image. ### Every selected prime is 5 modulo 12

An equality of real cubic outputs gives equality of $Z$, so the two integral differences $v,w$ have equal cubes modulo each $q\mathcal O_K$. The quotient is $\mathbb F_q^d$ by complete splitting. Cubing permutes each coordinate because $\gcd(3,q-1)=1$. The Chinese remainder theorem over the distinct $q$ gives $v-w=Qh$, $h\in\mathcal O_K$. At each embedding, $|\sigma(v-w)|\le4T=Q/2$, so $|\sigma h|\le1/2$. A nonzero $h$ has nonzero integral norm. Consequently $v=w$, equality of $Z$ recovers the common sum, and half the sum plus or minus half the difference recovers both ordered coordinates. Every diagonal and off-diagonal input pair is included, hence $|P_-(A,A)|=N^2$. [First checkpoint §D.3, lines 498–546](https://github.com/hnt119/polynomial-image-compression/blob/bd1f2d663262821575eebdfa559b7c25f76b46ab/research/history-sources/quadratic_to_cubic_transfer_checkpoint.md#L498-L546)

### Every selected prime is 1 modulo 12

**Necessary congruence and local alternatives.** For equal $Z$, $v^3-w^3=3Q^2(s-s')$. Thus $v,w\in B(Q/4)$ and their cubes agree modulo $Q^2\mathcal O_K$. Let $\mathcal C$ contain all such ordered differences with $v\ne w$. At each of the $td$ prime ideals $\mathfrak p\mid Q$, a unit $v$ gives precisely the local alternatives $w/v=1,\zeta,\zeta^{-1}\pmod{\mathfrak p^2}$. The three simple cube roots lift uniquely since $q\ne3$. The singular case consists of $v,w\in\mathfrak p$, whose cubes automatically vanish modulo $\mathfrak p^2$. There are at most $4^{td}$ choices of local types. [Complementary checkpoint §§C–D.2, lines 124–365](https://github.com/hnt119/polynomial-image-compression/blob/bd1f2d663262821575eebdfa559b7c25f76b46ab/research/history-sources/complementary_prime_transfer_obstruction.md#L124-L365)

**Count per type.** Write $W_T,W_U,W_Z$ for the sums of $\log N\mathfrak p$ over identity, nonidentity-unit and singular positions. Normalize $a=(2W_T+W_Z)/d$ and $b=(2W_U+W_Z)/d$. Then $a+b=2\log Q$. The difference $v-w$ lies in the ideal of norm $e^{ad}$ with exponents two on identity positions and one on singular positions. Once $v-w$ is fixed, the sum $v+w$ lies in one coset of the ideal of norm $e^{bd}$ with exponents two on nonidentity positions and one on singular positions. Indeed the local sum is $(1+\zeta^{\pm1})/(1-\zeta^{\pm1})$ times the difference at the appropriate nonidentity position. Both sums and differences have sup norm at most $Q/2$. For an ideal of norm $J$, its distinct coset points are separated by at least $J^{1/d}$ in sup norm by norm divisibility. A diameter-$Q$ box therefore has at most $(1+Q/J^{1/d})^d$ such points. This proves per-type count at most $[(1+Qe^{-a})(1+Qe^{-b})]^d$.

If $a\ge\tfrac12\log Q$ and the type occurs off the diagonal, $e^{ad}\le |N_{K/\mathbb Q}(v-w)|\le(Q/2)^d$. Thus $2\le x=Qe^{-a}\le\sqrt Q$ and $Qe^{-b}=x^{-1}$. The last packing factor is at most $(2+x+x^{-1})^d\le(4\sqrt Q)^d$.

If $a<\tfrac12\log Q$, then $h=(W_U+W_Z)/d=\log Q-W_T/d>\tfrac34\log Q$. Subtract any two distinct pairs of this type and denote the coordinate differences by $V,W$. The algebraic integer $L=V^2+VW+W^2$ belongs to $\mathfrak p^2$ at each nonidentity or singular position. At a nonidentity position both points use the same lifted root, so $W\equiv\zeta^{\pm1}V$, and at a singular position both differences belong to $\mathfrak p$. The real form is positive definite, so $L$ is totally positive and $N(L)\ge e^{2dh}$. Arithmetic-geometric mean gives $\sum_\sigma\sigma(L)\ge d e^{2h}$. Since $L(X,Y)\le\tfrac32(X^2+Y^2)$, the Euclidean separation in $2d$ dimensions is at least $r=\sqrt{2d/3}\,e^h$. The box for the original pair is contained in a ball of radius $R=Q\sqrt{d/8}$. Disjoint balls of radius $r/2$ give at most $(1+2R/r)^{2d}\le(1+(\sqrt3/2)Q^{1/4})^{2d}\le(4\sqrt Q)^d$ points. The last step uses $Q^{1/4}\ge1$ and $1+\sqrt3/2<2$. After summing over the four-way types this gives $|\mathcal C|\le(4^{t+1}\sqrt Q)^d$, including singular positions. [Complementary checkpoint §D.3, lines 366–476](https://github.com/hnt119/polynomial-image-compression/blob/bd1f2d663262821575eebdfa559b7c25f76b46ab/research/history-sources/complementary_prime_transfer_obstruction.md#L366-L476)

**From differences to the full image.** Equal differences in a collision force equal sums and exactly $N^2$ identical-input-pair collisions. For one ordered $(v,w)\in\mathcal C$, the first anchor has at most $N$ choices. It determines the first pair, then $s'=s+(w^3-v^3)/(3Q^2)$ and the second pair. Admissibility can only reduce this count. With multiplicities $m_z$ of actual real outputs,
$$
 E=\sum_zm_z^2\le N^2+N|\mathcal C|\le N^2(1+\lambda^d),\qquad
 \lambda=\frac{32\sqrt D\,4^t}{\sqrt Q}=e^{-\delta}.
$$
The factor 32 is $8\cdot4$, from the input lower bound and per-type packing. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $N^4=(\sum_zm_z)^2\le |P_-(A,A)|E$. Thus $|P_-(A,A)|\ge N^2/(1+e^{-\delta d})$, where $\delta=\tfrac12\log Q-t\log4-\log(32\sqrt D)$. The same energy bound shows that at most $N^2 e^{-\delta d}$ ordered input pairs belong to nonsingleton fibers since $m\le m(m-1)$ for $m\ge2$. [Complementary checkpoint §E, lines 478–580](https://github.com/hnt119/polynomial-image-compression/blob/bd1f2d663262821575eebdfa559b7c25f76b46ab/research/history-sources/complementary_prime_transfer_obstruction.md#L478-L580)

The $i$-th selected prime is at least $12i+1$, so $Q>12^t t!$. Substituting in $\lambda^2=1024D16^t/Q$ gives $\lambda^2<(256/41)(8/3)^t/t!$. The right side at $t=8$ is exactly $33554432/84735315<1$, and the ratio of consecutive terms is $(8/3)/(t+1)<1$. Consequently $\delta>\tfrac12\log(41t!/[256(8/3)^t])>0$. The bound also applies whenever $\delta>0$ is known directly. With fixed successful parameters and growing inputs, the image fraction tends to one, preventing a fixed exponent saving for this witness. For any fixed old scalar $a$, the degree-nine expression $P_-(f_\epsilon,a)$ is $(36i^3+3\epsilon i+2-a)^3-3(f_\epsilon+a)$. The reversed orientation negates exactly these high coefficients, since the linear part has degree at most three. Thus orientation, sign and old scalar are determined successively. These degree-nine polynomials cannot coincide identically with a new cubic or an old constant. Comparison of the new cubics with one another and with constants gives nonzero polynomials as well. The one intended constant equality is the repeated $-12$. Every unintended equality excludes finitely many real roots and hence finitely many integer indices. The designated pairs occupy a fraction $1/(4N)$ of $A_N^2$.
