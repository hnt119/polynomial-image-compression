## External field input and fixed parameters

The original *Planar Point Sets with Many Unit Distances*, Proposition 3.8 on printed and PDF page 12, was directly inspected. For each sufficiently large integer \(\ell\), put \(t=\lfloor(\ell-1)^2/100\rfloor\). P1 gives a totally real cyclic cubic base \(F\) with \(\log\operatorname{rd}(F)=O(\ell\log\ell)\). P2 gives finite everywhere-unramified Galois \(3\)-power extensions \(F_j/F\) of unbounded absolute degrees \(d_j\). P3 gives total reality and \(\operatorname{rd}(F_j)=\operatorname{rd}(F)=D\). P4 supplies \(t\) distinct rational primes \(p_i\equiv1\bmod 4\), fixed independently of \(j\), splitting completely in every layer. These are simultaneous choices. In the source, \(q_b\) denotes the selected prime and \(f_j\) the degree. The additional fields \(K_j=F_j(i)\) belong to the unit-distance construction. Remark 3.9 on page 14 confirms the order of choices there. The original PDF has SHA-256 `62b5de8cacdfbc0f13ae1940aa81659cd233e6945b936612fbeab0ab8ab3d383`. It writes the linear forms as \(\ell_1,\ell_2\), corresponding to \(L,M\) in November. Once the coefficients are fixed, so are \(H_P=(|a|+|b|)^2+|c|+|e|\) and \(C_P=2H_P+1\). For the supplied primes define \(\rho=\prod_i(p_i+1)/(2p_i)\). Each factor is at most \(3/5\), so

## Check of the full square

1. **Transport and scalar growth.** Let \(B\) be the integers of \(F_j\) bounded in every real embedding by \(X\). Equation (9) gives \(A_j=\iota_j(B)/R\) and \(n_j=|B|\) by injectivity. For \(P_R=L^2+RM\), equation (10) follows from \(P_R(Rx,Ry)=R^2P(x,y)\), also when \(L\) or \(M\) vanishes. In §3.3 (printed pp. 10–11, PDF pp. 12–13), the average lattice count in a translate of a half-open cube of side \(X\) is \(X^{d_j}/D^{d_j/2}\). Subtracting one occupied lattice point gives that many distinct points in the centered box, including zero. Distinct algebraic integers have a nonzero integral norm of their difference. Hence one conjugate differs by at least one, and side-one cubes have disjoint interiors. Together these give \((X/\sqrt D)^{d_j}\le n_j\le(2X+1)^{d_j}\), so \(n_j\to\infty\). [Deformation](https://github.com/hnt119/polynomial-image-compression/blob/c689ab52ca3bb9e3221bd09ae9a083a3111e39df/research/proofs/c0-v5/theorem-and-proof.tex#L42-L48) · [box count](https://github.com/hnt119/polynomial-image-compression/blob/c689ab52ca3bb9e3221bd09ae9a083a3111e39df/research/proofs/c0-v5/theorem-and-proof.tex#L91-L109)
2. **Residues and archimedean cost.** At an odd \(p\mid R\), one nonzero coefficient of \(L\bmod p\) gives the full \((p+1)/2\) squares on the residue plane. If both coefficients reduce to zero, the only output there is zero. Thus every actual box output lies in one of at most \((\rho R)^{d_j}\) CRT classes. They are classes in a product of copies of \(\mathbb F_p\). Every conjugate lies in \([-H,H]\), \(H=H_PX^2\), because \(R<X\). Within each class a nonzero difference is \(R\gamma\) with \(\gamma\) integral, so vectors are at least \(R\) apart in the maximum norm. Side-\(R\) cube packing bounds each class by \((2H/R+1)^{d_j}\), also for \(H=0\). This covers every ordered pair, irrespective of which square residues the finite box actually meets. V6 §3.4, printed pp. 11–12 (PDF pp. 13–14). [Residue and packing proof](https://github.com/hnt119/polynomial-image-compression/blob/c689ab52ca3bb9e3221bd09ae9a083a3111e39df/research/proofs/c0-v5/theorem-and-proof.tex#L111-L161)
3. **Normalization and exponent.** The two counts give \([\rho(2H+R)]^{d_j}\le (C_P\rho X^2)^{d_j}\) since \(R<X^2\). The lower scalar count gives \(X^{2d_j}\le D^{d_j}n_j^2\), hence the complete image has size at most \(\theta^{d_j} n_j^2\). The common input scaling and output scaling \(R^{-2}\iota_j\) preserve distinct cardinalities. The upper scalar bound gives \(d_j\ge\log n_j/\log(2X+1)\). Because \(\theta<1\), \(\theta^{d_j}\le n_j^{-\varepsilon_P}\), where \(\varepsilon_P=-\log\theta/\log(2X+1)>0\). There is no asymptotic error term or prefactor here. The mean fiber over attained outputs is \(n_j^2/|P(A_j,A_j)|\ge n_j^{\varepsilon_P}\). V6 §§3.4–3.5, printed p. 12 (PDF p. 14), equations (15)–(16). [Complete-image estimate and endpoint](https://github.com/hnt119/polynomial-image-compression/blob/c689ab52ca3bb9e3221bd09ae9a083a3111e39df/research/proofs/c0-v5/theorem-and-proof.tex#L150-L195)
4. **Determinants and edge cases.** The coefficient-row determinant \(ae-bc\) tests independence of \(L,M\). The homogeneous quadratic matrix is \((a,b)^{\mathsf T}(a,b)\), with determinant zero and rank one for \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\). With \(ab\ne0\), an additive exceptional representation must have quadratic outer and affine inner polynomials. Its linear part is proportional to \(L\), forcing \(ae-bc=0\). Conversely proportionality gives \(P=L^2+\lambda L\). A multiplicative representation with both variables present would force all three degrees to be one and its quadratic part to be proportional to \(xy\). This excludes it when \(ab\ne0\). If exactly one of \(a,b\) is zero the counting proof still applies, including to \(x^2+y\). If both vanish, an interval of \(n\) consecutive integers gives at most \((|c|+|e|)(n-1)+1\) outputs, exactly one for the zero polynomial. For \((1,2,3,-1)\) the two determinants are \(-7,0\), \(H_P=13\), \(C_P=27\), and the single illustrative prime \(5\) gives the local image \(\{0,1,4\}\). The theorem concerns the displayed integral square-plus-linear family. V6 §3.4 and §3.6, printed pp. 12–13 (PDF pp. 14–15).
