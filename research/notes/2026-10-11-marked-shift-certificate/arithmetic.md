T01, 11 October 2026, version 1. The fixed target polynomial remains

**PROVED — MS-C1.** For the exact positive real constants and marked
points of TC-G5, the point \(T=R_2-R_1\) has infinite order.
The proof uses one preselected rational prime, 23, and a local
torsion-free subgroup. It does not test increasing possible orders.

## 1. The exact point and its fixed field

Put
\[
 a=\sqrt2,\quad \lambda=\frac{3+a}{14},\quad H=\sqrt\lambda>0,
 \quad b=4a-3,\quad W=\sqrt b>0,
\]
\[
 h_1=(a-1)H,\quad h_2=H,\quad s_1=HW,\quad s_2=(a-1)H,
 \quad c=8H(1-2\lambda).
\]
These are exactly TC-G5's constants, without replacing the curve or its
marked point. The two equal expressions for c were proved there. Here
\[
 c^2=\frac{64(19-3a)}{343},\qquad
 E:\ Y^2=X^3-48X-128-432c^2.                              \tag{1}
\]
The positive-Y marked points, written in the same short coordinates as
TC-G5, are
\[
 R_1=\left(\frac{68+144a}{7},
             \frac{288HW(2+3a)}7\right),\qquad
 R_2=\left(\frac{164-48a}{7},
             \frac{288H(5a-6)}7\right).                  \tag{2}
\]
They follow by substituting the widths into
\(R_i=(44-96h_i^2,288s_i(1-2h_i^2))\), using a^2=2.
Their membership in (1) is also an immediate substitution, as already
checked by the preceding exact audit. The difference between their
X-coordinates is nonzero.

An explicit chord formula specifies T itself without an implicit choice
of a division point. Let
\[
 m_T=\frac{3H\bigl((5a-6)+W(2+3a)\bigr)}{1-2a},
\]
\[
 X_T=m_T^2-\frac{232+96a}{7},\qquad
 Y_T=m_T\left(\frac{164-48a}{7}-X_T\right)
                         -\frac{288H(5a-6)}7.             \tag{3}
\]
Then T=(X_T,Y_T): the chord joins R2 and -R1. Its denominator 1-2a
does not vanish. All signs agree with the commissioned T=R2-R1.

**PROVED field statement.** The field
\[
 F_0=\mathbb Q(a),\quad F=F_0(H),\quad K=F_0(H,W)           \tag{4}
\]
has degrees 2,4,8 respectively over Q. It is the exact joint coordinate
field of the two marked points and their curve. Formula (3) puts T in
E(K); no claim that K is the minimal field generated by T alone is
needed.

Indeed
\[
 N_{F_0/\mathbb Q}(\lambda)=1/28,\quad
 N_{F_0/\mathbb Q}(b)=-23,\quad
 N_{F_0/\mathbb Q}(b/\lambda)=-644.                        \tag{5}
\]
The first is not a rational square, so lambda is not a square in F0.
The last two are negative, so neither b nor b/lambda is a square in F0.
If b were a square in F=F0(H), write its square root as x+yH with x,y
in F0. The H coefficient in (x+yH)^2 forces 2xy=0. This would make
b a square in F0 or b/lambda a square in F0, both impossible. Thus
the indicated extensions have the asserted degrees. Conversely the
two X-coordinates in (2) recover a, the second Y-coordinate then recovers
H, and the first recovers W. Hence the joint coordinate field is K.

There is consequently a well-defined automorphism
\[
 \sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/F),\quad
 \sigma(W)=-W,\quad \sigma(a)=a,\quad \sigma(H)=H.         \tag{6}
\]
It fixes the coefficients of E and satisfies
\[
 \sigma(R_1)=-R_1,\quad \sigma(R_2)=R_2,\quad
 T+\sigma(T)=2R_2.                                       \tag{7}
\]
Thus it suffices to prove that R2 has infinite order. This uses an
automorphism of a proved degree-eight field, not an assumed independent
change of square-root signs.

 m_{R_2}=H(16a-4),\qquad

For completeness, the previously established tangent formula is

These are identities in the fixed field F, not approximations.

## 3. The one local embedding and good reduction

Use p=23 and normalize v23(23)=1. There is an embedding
\(\iota:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb Q_{23}\) with
\[
 a\equiv18=-5\pmod{23},\qquad H\equiv6\pmod{23}.          \tag{9}
\]
Here and below the same letters denote their images under this embedding;
it need not preserve the chosen ordering of the original real field.

All root-lifting hypotheses can be checked directly. The equation
a^2=2 has the simple residue root 18, since 18^2-2 is divisible by 23
and 2*18 is nonzero modulo 23. Once a is lifted, lambda=(3+a)/14 is
a 23-adic unit with residue13. The equation H^2=lambda has the simple
residue root6, since 6^2=13 modulo23 and 2*6 is nonzero. To justify
the lifting step explicitly, if a root is known modulo23^n and its
derivative is a unit, adding 23^n times a residue digit uniquely cancels
the next error modulo23^(n+1). These compatible lifts converge in Z23
to a root. The two successive roots define the asserted embedding of
the field F into Q23. No completion containing W is required.

The curve (1) has coefficients in Z23. Its discriminant is
\[
 \Delta=-2^{12}3^6c^2(16+27c^2).                           \tag{10}
\]
At (9), direct reduction gives
\[
 \lambda\equiv13,\quad c^2\equiv16,\quad
 16+27c^2\equiv11\pmod{23}.                              \tag{11}
\]
Neither factor on the right of (10) vanishes modulo23. Hence this
integral short Weierstrass equation has good reduction at the chosen
place; no minimal-model calculation or bad-reduction exception is being
suppressed.

## 5. The all-orders local criterion, with proof

**PROVED local lemma.** Let p>=5, and let
\(E_0:y^2=x^3+Ax+B\) have A,B in Zp and discriminant a p-adic unit.
If a finite point V in E0(Qp) has vp(x(V))<0, then V has infinite order.

This is the local lemma of EG-C2 in
[complex-extensions.md](../2026-09-30-elliptic-geometry/complex-extensions.md),
read and applied with its hypotheses checked in §§3–4. The proof is
included so the all-orders conclusion is available without a torsion
table or an external rank computation.

Use local coordinates t=-x/y and w=-1/y at O. The equation becomes
\[
 w=t^3+Atw^2+Bw^3.
\]
The coefficient of w in the implicit equation is a unit at (0,0), so
recursive comparison yields
\(w(t)\in t^3\mathbb Z_p[[t]]\), with w=t^3+O(t^7). The smooth
integral Weierstrass group law is regular at O; in this parameter its
expansion is
\[
 F(t_1,t_2)\in\mathbb Z_p[[t_1,t_2]],\qquad
 F(t_1,t_2)=t_1+t_2+\text{terms of degree at least two}.
\]
Consequently parameters in pZp form the identity subgroup. The invariant
differential has integral expansion
\[
 \frac{dx}{2y}=\frac{t w'(t)-w(t)}{2w(t)}\,dt
       =\left(1+\sum_{n\ge1}a_n t^n\right)dt,
       \qquad a_n\in\mathbb Z_p.
\]
For clarity, invariance follows from the chord law: for a fixed point
(r,s), let m=(y-s)/(x-r), x'=m^2-x-r and
y'=-y+m(x-x'). The curve equations imply
\[
 3x^2+A-2my=(x-r)(2x+r-m^2),
\]
and differentiation gives dx'/(2y')=dx/(2y). Regularity extends the
identity across the exceptional chord denominators.

Integrating the displayed power series gives
\[
 \ell(t)=t+\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{a_n}{n+1}t^{n+1}.
\]
For t in pZp with vp(t)=r>=1, the terms converge and every term after
t has valuation at least
\[
 (n+1)r-v_p(n+1)>r.
\]
To check the strict inequality for every n, put k=vp(n+1). The bounds
\(p^k\le n+1\) and \(p^k\ge1+(p-1)k\) give
\(v_p(n+1)\le n/(p-1)<n\). Since r>=1, the claimed strict
inequality follows. Thus vp(ell(t))=r and ell(t) is nonzero for t nonzero. Invariance of
the differential and F(t,0)=t give
\(\ell(F(t_1,t_2))=\ell(t_1)+\ell(t_2)\). A nonzero finite-order
parameter would imply n ell(t)=0 in the characteristic-zero field Qp,
which is impossible.

Finally, if vp(x)=-a<0, the term x^3 strictly dominates Ax and B.
The curve equation gives 2vp(y)=-3a, so a=2r for an integer r>=1,
and vp(t)=r. This point lies in the just-proved torsion-free identity
subgroup. The lemma follows. No assertion about all torsion points
outside this subgroup is needed. \(\square\)

Applying the lemma at p=23 to U in (14) proves U has infinite order.
Therefore S=2R2 and R2 also have infinite order. If the commissioned
T had finite order, its field conjugate sigma(T) would have finite
order as well, with the same annihilating integer. Equation (7) would
make S=T+sigma(T) torsion, a contradiction. This proves MS-C1 and
resolves TC-O1 for every possible order. \(\square\)
