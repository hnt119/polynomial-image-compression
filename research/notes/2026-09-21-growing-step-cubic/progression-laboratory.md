# Arithmetic progressions: all steps and all actual fibers

2026-09-21, v1. T01, \(P(x,y)=(x-y)^3+x+y\).
New elementary proofs for the authorized offline growing-step cycle.
No numerical code, network source, or arithmetic amplification was used.
The inherited coordinates are
\[
u=x-y,\quad z=P(x,y),\qquad
x=\frac{z-u^3+u}{2},\quad y=\frac{z-u^3-u}{2}.
\]
The map \(T_\tau\) changes \(u\) to \(u+\tau\) and fixes \(z\).
Graphs below are undirected; positive steps count each edge once.
Common real translation of a carrier preserves all return and graph
counts, so all displayed carriers may be translated.

## 1. GS-A1 — two progression families already have connected fibers

**Meaning.** Adding more steps to the integer and third-integer intervals
adds edges inside components already connected by \(T_1\). Every such
component is an entire actual cubic fiber. Thus the component count
equals the actual image size, rather than merely bounding it.

**PROVED.** For
\[
A_{r,N}=\{j/r:0\le j<N\},\qquad r\in\{1,3\},\quad N\ge1,
\]
each nonempty fiber \(U_{A_{r,N}}(z)\) is a finite interval in one coset
of \(\mathbb Z\):
\[
U_{A_{r,N}}(z)=\{u_0,u_0+1,\ldots,u_0+\ell-1\}.
\tag{A1}
\]
Every return step is an integer. For every integer \(M\ge1\), the
components of \(G(A_{r,N},\{1,\ldots,M\})\) are the actual fibers.
No real step inventory can merge these components. In particular,
\[
|P(A_{r,N},A_{r,N})|=N^2-E_1(A_{r,N}).
\tag{A2}
\]

**Proof.** For \(r=1\), differences and return steps are integers.
For \(r=3\), write \(x=i/3,\ y=j/3,\ d=i-j\). Then
\[
27P(x,y)=d^3+9(i+j).
\]
Cubes modulo nine are respectively \(0,1,-1\) when their arguments
are \(0,1,-1\) modulo three. Thus equal outputs force their difference
indices to agree modulo three; their real differences differ by an integer.
This proves the one-coset and step assertions.

The map \(T_1\) preserves the ambient third-integer lattice because
\[
3c(d/3)=\frac{d^2+3d+6}{2}\in\mathbb Z,
\qquad c(u)=\frac{3u^2+3u+2}{2}.
\]
It also preserves the integer lattice, since \(c(u)\) is integral there.
Thus every intermediate unit translate of an actual fiber point has
both coordinates in the appropriate ambient lattice.

The smaller and larger recovered coordinates are
\[
\frac{z-F_+(u)}2,\quad\frac{z-F_-(u)}2,
\qquad F_\pm(u)=u^3\pm|u|.
\tag{A3}
\]
Both \(F_\pm\) are nondecreasing under \(u\mapsto u+1\) on
\(\frac13\mathbb Z\). Outside \([-1,0]\), this follows from
\((u+1)^3-u^3\ge1\) and \(\bigl||u+1|-|u|\bigr|=1\).
Across \([-1,0]\), their increments are
\[
(3u+2)(u+1),\qquad u(3u+1),
\]
both nonnegative at the only relevant grid points
\(-1,-2/3,-1/3,0\).
Hence both coordinate extremes in (A3) are nonincreasing along the
unit-spaced coset. Requiring the smaller to be at least zero gives an
initial segment; requiring the larger to be at most \((N-1)/r\)
gives a final segment. Their intersection is an interval. Ambient
lattice preservation then proves (A1).

A fiber of size \(\ell\) has exactly \(\ell-1\) \(T_1\) edges.
Summing over fibers, whose total mass is \(N^2\), proves (A2).
All other steps preserve the output and cannot join different fibers.
\(\square\)

**Why it matters and scope.** This is an all-steps obstruction for these
carriers. It uses the proved interval property, not the prompt's general
assertion that components equal intersections with cosets of the subgroup
generated by the steps. That assertion is false for a general finite
fiber because intermediate endpoints may be absent.

## 2. GS-A2 — exact counts for every integer step

**Meaning.** A step \(k\) spans \(k\) places inside an interval fiber.
Equivalently, count the anchors for which its source and destination
pairs fit into the scalar progression.

**PROVED.** Write \(E_k=E_k(A_{r,N})\), \(r=1\) or \(3\).
Noninteger steps have zero returns, and \(E_{-k}=E_k\).
For integer \(k\ge1\), define
\[
b_{r,k}(d)=\frac{3kd^2}{2r}+\frac{3k^2d}{2}
             +\frac{r(k^3+k)}2,
\tag{A4}
\]
\[
w_{r,k}(d)=b_{r,k}(d)+\max(0,d)-\min(0,d+rk).
\]
For integer \(d\) these quantities are integral, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_k&=\sum_{d\in\mathbb Z}(N-w_{r,k}(d))_+\\
&=2\sum_{d\ge0}(N-d-b_{r,k}(d))_+
  +\sum_{d=-rk+1}^{-1}(N-b_{r,k}(d))_+.
\end{aligned}
\tag{A5}
\]
These sums have finite support. For every fixed \(k\ge1\),
\[
E_k=\frac43\sqrt{\frac{2r}{3k}}\,N^{3/2}+O_{r,k}(N).
\tag{A6}
\]

**Proof.** Write the source pair as \(((j+d)/r,j/r)\). The second
coordinate index after \(T_k\) is \(j-b_{r,k}(d)\), and the new
difference index is \(d+rk\). Indeed (A4) expands
\[
\frac r2\left[\left(\frac dr+k\right)^3
                 -\left(\frac dr\right)^3+k\right].
\]
Integrality follows also from ambient lattice preservation and
\(T_k=T_1^k\). The four scalar-index offsets from \(j\) are
\[
0,\quad d,\quad -b_{r,k}(d),\quad d+rk-b_{r,k}(d).
\]
By the monotonicity in GS-A1, the largest source coordinate is at least
the largest destination coordinate, and the smallest destination
coordinate is at most the smallest source coordinate. The total span
is therefore \(w_{r,k}(d)\), giving exactly \((N-w_{r,k}(d))_+\) anchors.

The substitution \(d\mapsto-d-rk\) preserves both \(b\) and \(w\).
The span is \(d+b\) for \(d\ge0\), and \(b\) for \(-rk<d<0\).
This proves (A5). The inverse map reverses positive-step edges,
proving \(E_{-k}=E_k\).
For fixed \(r,k\), the positive-index summand has the form
\[
\left(N-\frac{3k}{2r}d^2-O_{r,k}(d+1)\right)_+.
\]
Its cutoff is \(\sqrt{2rN/(3k)}+O_{r,k}(1)\). Summing \(d,d^2\)
to this cutoff gives (A6); the middle \(rk-1\) indices contribute
\(O_{r,k}(N)\). \(\square\)

**Why it matters.** The \(k=1\) count and GS-A1 give actual-image formulas
\[
|P(A_{1,N},A_{1,N})|
=N^2-\frac43\sqrt{\frac23}\,N^{3/2}+O(N),
\tag{A7}
\]
\[
|P(A_{3,N},A_{3,N})|
=N^2-\frac{4\sqrt2}{3}N^{3/2}+O(N).
\tag{A8}
\]
Both image densities tend to one, excluding any fixed positive power
saving for these carriers.

**Scope.** The error in (A6) is for fixed \(k\). Formula (A5) is exact
for growing \(k\); uniform growing-step bounds follow next.

## 3. GS-A3 — full component distribution and redundant edge growth

**Meaning.** Return counts determine every component size. Additional
steps increase the edge count while leaving components and covered
mass unchanged.

**PROVED.** Put \(E_0=N^2\). For either \(A_{r,N}\), let \(n_\ell\)
be the number of actual fibers with \(\ell\) pairs. For every
\(M\ge1\), these are also the component counts for steps
\(\{1,\ldots,M\}\), and
\[
n_\ell=E_{\ell-1}-2E_\ell+E_{\ell+1}.
\tag{A9}
\]
The total component count is \(N^2-E_1\); the mass in components of size
at least \(\ell\) is
\[
B_\ell=\ell E_{\ell-1}-(\ell-1)E_\ell.
\tag{A10}
\]
In particular, the exact nonisolated mass is
\[
B_2=2E_1-E_2
=\frac43\sqrt{\frac r3}(2\sqrt2-1)N^{3/2}+O(N).
\tag{A11}
\]
This is its maximum over every real step inventory.

The maximum component size is \(K_r(N)+1\), where
\[
K_r(N)=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{k\ge1:N>m_r(k)\}\bigr),
\]
\[
m_1(k)=
\begin{cases}(k^3+4k)/8,&k\ \mathrm{even},\\
(k^3+7k)/8,&k\ \mathrm{odd},\end{cases}
\quad
m_3(k)=
\begin{cases}(3k^3+12k)/8,&k\ \mathrm{even},\\
(3k^3+13k)/8,&k\ \mathrm{odd}.
\end{cases}
\tag{A12}
\]
Thus \(K_r(N)=2(N/r)^{1/3}+O(1)\).
The exact number of distinct undirected edges is
\[
\mathcal E_M=\sum_{k=1}^M E_k.
\tag{A13}
\]
Uniformly over integers \(M\ge1\), as \(N\to\infty\),
\[
\mathcal E_M
=\Theta_r\left(N^{3/2}\sqrt{\min\{M,N^{1/3}\}}\right).
\tag{A14}
\]
At \(M\ge K_r(N)\), every actual fiber is a complete graph and the
edge count saturates at \(\Theta_r(N^{5/3})\). The component
distribution was already attained at \(M=1\).

**Proof.** A fiber with \(j\) points contributes \((j-k)_+\) returns
at step \(k\). Hence \(E_k=\sum_j(j-k)_+n_j\), including \(k=0\).
Taking the second difference proves (A9). The first difference is
\(\sum_{j\ge\ell}n_j=E_{\ell-1}-E_\ell\); adding the excess
vertices \(E_\ell=\sum_{j\ge\ell}(j-\ell)n_j\) proves (A10).
Using (A6) at \(k=1,2\) gives (A11).
No inventory can connect singleton actual fibers to anything else,
whereas \(T_1\) already connects every nonsingleton fiber.

Complete the square:
\[
b_{r,k}(d)=
\frac{3k}{2r}\left(d+\frac{rk}{2}\right)^2
 +\frac{rk^3}{8}+\frac{rk}{2}.
\tag{A15}
\]
The additional terms in \(w-b\) are nonnegative and vanish for
\(-rk\le d\le0\), which contains the quadratic's minimum.
Choosing the nearest integer to \(-rk/2\) gives (A12).
Thus \(E_k>0\) exactly when \(N>m_r(k)\), equivalently when some
interval fiber has at least \(k+1\) points. This proves the longest-size
statement.

Distinct positive steps connect distinct unordered pairs, proving
(A13). A contributing index in (A15) satisfies
\[
\left|d+\frac{rk}{2}\right|<\sqrt{\frac{2rN}{3k}}.
\]
There are \(O_r(\sqrt{N/k}+1)\) such indices, each with at most \(N\)
anchors; all steps beyond \(K_r(N)=O_r(N^{1/3})\) contribute zero.
Summing proves the upper bound in (A14).

For the lower bound, choose sufficiently small fixed \(c_0,c_1>0\),
depending only on \(r\). If
\[
1\le k\le c_0N^{1/3},\qquad 0\le d\le c_1\sqrt{N/k},
\]
then \(d+b_{r,k}(d)\le N/2\) for all sufficiently large \(N\).
Indeed the terms \(kd^2,k^2d,k^3\) are bounded by constant multiples
of \(c_1^2N,c_1c_0^{3/2}N,c_0^3N\), and the remaining terms are
uniformly \(o(N)\). Thus \(E_k\gg_r N^{3/2}/\sqrt k\).
Summing to \(\min\{M,\lfloor c_0N^{1/3}\rfloor\}\) proves the lower
bound. When \(M\) exceeds this range, that partial sum is already
of order \(N^{5/3}\). \(\square\)

**Why it matters.** No \(M\) gives quadratic coverage: only
\(\Theta(N^{3/2})\) of the \(N^2\) pairs lie in nonsingleton actual
fibers. A growing inventory creates redundant edges, not additional
component reduction. Formula (A10) also charges every vertex outside
any requested component-size threshold.

**Scope.** These identities depend on interval fibers. They do not
hold for an arbitrary scalar set with gaps or multiple components
inside one actual fiber.

## 4. GS-A4 — all fixed real spacings have almost \(N^2\) outputs

**Meaning.** A weaker all-steps obstruction holds for every fixed-spacing
AP. An output collision cannot use a sufficiently large index difference.
If the spacing squared is irrational, there are no collisions at all.

**PROVED.** For \(h>0,\ t\in\mathbb R\), put
\(A=t+h\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\) and
\[
D_{h,N}=\min\left\{N-1,
\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{2(N-1)}}h\right\rfloor\right\}.
\]
The number of pairs in nonsingleton actual fibers is at most
\(\min\{N^2,N(2D_{h,N}+1)\}\). Hence
\[
N^2-\min\{N^2,N(2D_{h,N}+1)\}\le |P(A,A)|\le N^2.
\tag{A16}
\]
For fixed \(h\), this gives \(|P(A,A)|=N^2-O_h(N^{3/2})\);
every step inventory has only \(O_h(N^{3/2})\) nonisolated vertices.
If \(h^2\) is irrational, the image is exactly \(N^2\).

**Proof.** With \(d=i-j\),
\[
\frac{P(t+hi,t+hj)-2t}{h}=h^2d^3+i+j.
\tag{A17}
\]
Equality of two outputs implies
\[
h^2(d^3-e^3)=(i'+j')-(i+j),\qquad
|h^2(d^3-e^3)|\le2(N-1).
\tag{A18}
\]
If \(d=e\), the sums also agree and the pairs are identical. For
distinct integer \(d,e\), the nearest distinct cube is at distance at least
\[
3d^2-3|d|+1\ge d^2.
\]
The last inequality follows from
\((2|d|-1)(|d|-1)\ge0\) for \(|d|\ge1\); \(d=0\) is immediate.
Thus any pair in a nonsingleton fiber has \(|d|\le D_{h,N}\).
Each difference supports at most \(N\) pairs. Every remaining pair
gives its own singleton output, proving (A16).
If \(h^2\) is irrational, (A18) cannot hold with a nonzero integer
\(d^3-e^3\), so every fiber is a singleton. \(\square\)

**Why it matters and scope.** This excludes growing-step coverage for
all fixed real AP spacings. The bound explicitly charges a varying
\(h\); it gives \(o(N^2)\) nontrivial mass if \(h\sqrt N\to\infty\),
but may be uninformative at spacing of order \(N^{-1/2}\) or smaller.
It does not assert interval fibers for arbitrary \(h\), or treat a
bounded union of APs as one interval. The separate general lattice
obstruction applies when its spacing and diameter hypotheses hold.

For completeness, a general AP has an exact anchor formula for all steps.
If \(\tau/h\notin\mathbb Z\), then \(E_\tau=0\). If \(\tau=hk\),
\(k\in\mathbb Z\), let
\[
B_{h,k}(d)=\frac{h^2((d+k)^3-d^3)+k}{2}.
\]
Only \(d\) with \(B_{h,k}(d)\in\mathbb Z\) contribute. The exact
count for that difference is \((N-W_{h,k}(d))_+\), where \(W\) is
the span of
\[
\{0,d,-B_{h,k}(d),d+k-B_{h,k}(d)\}.
\tag{A19}
\]
Summing over \(d\in\mathbb Z\) gives \(E_\tau\). This follows by writing
the four scalar indices; it assumes no intermediate-step membership.
