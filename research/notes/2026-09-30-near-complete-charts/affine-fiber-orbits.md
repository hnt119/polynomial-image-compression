# Finite invariant sets under nonlinear pair maps

T01 / near-complete nonlinear charts, 2026-09-30 v1.
The fixed polynomial is \(P(x,y)=(x-y)^3+x+y\).
This is new offline symbolic research with **zero carrier evaluations**.
No earlier search is rerun. Proofs are internal; no external theorem or
novelty claim is used. Independent internal lead audit of NC-A1–A5 is
complete. This is not external review.

For orientation, the familiar motion \(u\mapsto u+\tau\) preserves an
output fiber. A finite segment of that motion can have many points, but
the nonzero translation cannot permute that entire finite segment.
The distinction between partial returns and a map preserving a whole
finite controlled set is decisive below.

We use the exact polynomial change of coordinates
\[
H(x,y)=(u,z)=(x-y,P(x,y)),\qquad
H^{-1}(u,z)=\left(\frac{z-u^3+u}{2},\frac{z-u^3-u}{2}\right).
\tag{1}
\]
For a finite controlled set \(E\subseteq A^2\), put \(Q=|E|\),
\(N=|A|\), and \(E_z=\{u:H^{-1}(u,z)\in E\}\).
The sets \(E_z\) are actual finite fiber sections, not unrestricted
products of difference and output parameters.

## 1. NC-A1 — all polynomial automorphisms preserving the cubic

### Meaning

A globally invertible polynomial pair map can look highly nonlinear in
\(x,y\). If it preserves the cubic output exactly, however, its action
on the difference within each output fiber is only affine.

### Exact statement — PROVED

Let \(\Phi:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2\) be a polynomial automorphism:
both \(\Phi\) and its inverse have polynomial coordinates. Then
\[
P\circ\Phi=P
\]
if and only if, in coordinates (1),
\[
\boxed{H\circ\Phi\circ H^{-1}(u,z)=(a u+f(z),z)}
\tag{2}
\]
for a nonzero real constant \(a\) and a real polynomial \(f\).
There is no restriction on the degree of \(f\).

Equivalently, setting \(v=a(x-y)+f(P(x,y))\), its original coordinates are
\[
\Phi(x,y)=
\left(\frac{P(x,y)-v^3+v}{2},
      \frac{P(x,y)-v^3-v}{2}\right).
\tag{3}
\]

### Proof

Conjugation by (1) gives a polynomial automorphism of the form
\((F(u,z),z)\). Its inverse has the form \((G(u,z),z)\), since
preserving \(z\) also holds for the inverse. Thus
\[
G(F(u,z),z)=u.
\]
Both degrees in \(u\) are positive. Over the integral domain
\(\mathbb R[z]\), degree in \(u\) multiplies under this composition:
the product of the relevant leading coefficients is nonzero.
Consequently both degrees are one. Write
\[
F=a(z)u+b(z),\qquad G=c(z)u+d(z).
\]
Their composition gives \(c(z)a(z)=1\). The only polynomial units in
\(\mathbb R[z]\) are nonzero constants, so \(a\) is constant and
nonzero. This proves necessity. Conversely (2) has polynomial inverse
\(((u-f(z))/a,z)\); conjugating back proves sufficiency. \(\square\)

### Why it matters

This covers output-preserving polynomial automorphisms of arbitrary
degree, with nonlinear dependence on the output itself. It is substantially
larger than a fixed inventory of constant translations.

### Scope

A polynomial map whose inverse is merely a set-theoretic inverse is not
automatically covered. General polynomial endomorphisms and rational maps
with poles are also outside this classification. No finite Cartesian
membership is asserted by (2).

## 2. NC-A2 — an arbitrarily large invariant affine-fiber library has only two-point orbits

### Meaning

A finite ordered set admits only one order-preserving permutation:
the identity. Its only affine order-reversing permutation, when one
exists, is reversal around its midpoint. Adding more such rules cannot
make long equivalence classes.

### Exact statement — PROVED

Let \(A\subset\mathbb R\) be finite and \(E\subseteq A^2\). Consider
any family of output-preserving maps whose action on every occupied
fiber is
\[
u\longmapsto a_j(z)u+b_j(z),\qquad a_j(z)\ne0,
\tag{4}
\]
and assume that every map is defined at every point of \(E\) and maps
\(E\) into \(E\). The coefficients may depend arbitrarily on the
occupied outputs. In particular they may be rational functions of
\(z\), finite there. The family size and the coefficient degrees may grow.

Every equivalence class generated by these maps and their inverse
restrictions to \(E\) has size at most two. If \(C\) is the number of
such classes, then
\[
\boxed{C\ge\lceil Q/2\rceil.}
\tag{5}
\]
The standard certificate using one output per class and charging the
uncontrolled complement therefore has numerical cost
\[
\boxed{C+(N^2-Q)\ge N^2-\lfloor Q/2\rfloor\ge\lceil N^2/2\rceil.}
\tag{6}
\]

In particular, this architecture cannot produce the requested
\(N^\varepsilon\)-size equivalence classes on a near-complete controlled
set, regardless of the size or degree of its library.
Every family from NC-A1 satisfies its algebraic hypothesis.

### Proof

Each map in (4) is injective on a fiber. Because it maps the finite set
\(E_z\) into itself, it permutes \(E_z\).

Write \(E_z=\{u_1<\cdots<u_k\}\). If \(a_j(z)>0\), the permutation
preserves order and therefore fixes each \(u_i\). If \(a_j(z)<0\),
it reverses order and sends \(u_i\) to \(u_{k+1-i}\).
When \(k\ge2\), its endpoint equations give more precisely
\[
a_j(z)=-1,\qquad b_j(z)=u_1+u_k.
\]
When \(k=1\), every permutation fixes that point. Thus every generator
acts on the fiber as either identity or the same reversal. Compositions
and inverses do the same, so the orbit of \(u_i\) is contained in
\(\{u_i,u_{k+1-i}\}\). Summing over fibers proves (5), and (6)
follows by charging every pair outside \(E\). \(\square\)

### Why it matters

This is an obstruction to a **near-complete orbit certificate**, not
merely a capacity bound for a bounded number of curves. It survives
arbitrarily many output-dependent rules. In the polynomial case the
maps may have arbitrarily large degree.

### Scope

Equation (6) is a lower bound on this particular upper-bound certificate.
It is **not** an actual-image lower bound: different two-point classes
may still share an output, and proving such further sharing would require
another mechanism.

The common invariance hypothesis is essential. If maps are applied only
where they happen to return to \(E\), a finite translation path may be
long; that partial-return setting is not ruled out here. Nor does the
theorem cover a parameter-dependent choice of different affine formulas
at different points of one fiber, unless it comes from the displayed
common family with each map preserving all of \(E\).

## 3. NC-A3 — coordinate-preserving rules cannot create large output classes

### Meaning

Keeping a row fixed leaves at most three partners with the same cubic
output. Keeping a column fixed leaves just one. A globally rational
row rule is more rigid still: if it preserves the output identically,
it must be the identity.

### Exact statement — PROVED

For every finite \(E\subseteq A^2\), an equivalence relation generated
by any family of output-preserving rules, each of which fixes the first
coordinate or fixes the second coordinate, has classes of size at most
three. The rules need not be polynomial, rational, invertible, or given
by the same formula on different rows.

Rules fixing the second coordinate act identically. Thus the number
\(C_{\rm row}\) of generated classes satisfies
\[
C_{\rm row}\ge\lceil Q/3\rceil,\qquad
C_{\rm row}+N^2-Q\ge N^2-Q+\lceil Q/3\rceil
\ge\lceil N^2/3\rceil.
\tag{7}
\]

Moreover, for a real rational function \(R(x,y)\), either identity
\[
P(x,R(x,y))=P(x,y),\qquad
P(R(x,y),y)=P(x,y)
\tag{8}
\]
forces respectively \(R=y\) or \(R=x\).

### Proof

For fixed \(y\), the polynomial \(x\mapsto P(x,y)\) is strictly
increasing, with derivative \(3(x-y)^2+1>0\). Therefore a
second-coordinate-preserving output equality cannot change the first
coordinate. Such rules are identities.

A first-coordinate-preserving rule stays inside
\[
\{(x,y)\in E:P(x,y)=z\}
\]
for one fixed \(x,z\). As a polynomial in \(y\), \(P(x,y)-z\) has
degree three and nonzero leading coefficient. This set has at most
three points. Column rules add no edges, so the same bound holds after
combining all the rules, including their undirected equivalence closure.
Counting classes proves (7).

It remains to prove the rational strengthening for a fixed first
coordinate. Put \(u=x-y\) and \(v=x-R(x,y)\). The first identity in
(8) says
\[
(u-v)(u^2+uv+v^2-1)=0
\]
in the rational function field \(\mathbb R(x,u)\).
If \(u\ne v\), the second factor vanishes, hence
\[
(2v+u)^2=4-3u^2.
\tag{9}
\]
This is impossible for a rational function: view it as a rational
function of \(u\) with coefficient field \(\mathbb R(x)\).
The right side has a simple zero at \(u=2/\sqrt3\), whereas a square
of a nonzero rational function has even order at every zero or pole.
Thus \(u=v\) and \(R=y\).

For the second identity in (8), strict monotonicity gives \(R=x\)
at every real point where the rational function is defined. That set
contains a nonempty open set, so the rational functions are identical.
Alternatively the corresponding factor is
\((u-v)(u^2+uv+v^2+1)\), whose second factor is strictly positive
where defined. \(\square\)

### Why it matters

An arbitrarily large library of exact output-preserving row/column rules
cannot supply the long classes required by the prompt. Permitting
nonlinear rational formulas does not rescue the globally identical
row ansatz.

### Scope

As in NC-A2, (7) bounds an orbit/class certificate, not the number of
actual outputs. The claim does not cover pair maps changing both
coordinates, or row maps that change the output according to a new
rule. A reduction of transformed output support is not automatically
a reduction of the original cubic support.

## 4. NC-A4 — rational maps with poles really can have long finite orbits

### Meaning

The polynomial or affine restriction is substantive. A rational
transformation can rotate the extended real parameter line and have
arbitrarily long finite cycles. Those cycles do exist on cubic fibers.
Their direct scalar completion, however, is sparse.

### Exact statement — PROVED

For each integer \(m\ge3\), set
\[
\theta=\frac{\pi}{m},\qquad \alpha=\frac{\pi}{4m},\qquad
M_m(u)=\frac{\cos\theta\,u+\sin\theta}
            {-\sin\theta\,u+\cos\theta}.
\tag{10}
\]
Let
\[
u_j=\tan(\alpha+j\theta),\qquad 0\le j<m.
\]
These are \(m\) distinct finite real numbers, and \(M_m(u_j)=u_{j+1}\),
with indices modulo \(m\). Define the rational pair map
\[
\Psi_m=H^{-1}\circ[(u,z)\mapsto(M_m(u),z)]\circ H.
\tag{11}
\]
It preserves \(P\) wherever defined and cycles the finite set
\[
E_m=\left\{\left(\frac{-u_j^3+u_j}{2},
                       \frac{-u_j^3-u_j}{2}\right):0\le j<m\right\}.
\tag{12}
\]
If \(A_m\) is the union of its scalar coordinates and \(N_m=|A_m|\),
then
\[
\boxed{Q=m,\qquad m\le N_m\le2m,\qquad |P(E_m)|=1,\qquad
\frac{|E_m|}{N_m^2}\le\frac1m.}
\tag{13}
\]
The full-square completion certificate is only
\[
1+N_m^2-m=N_m^2-\Theta(N_m).
\tag{14}
\]
This does not determine the actual full image.

### Proof

The tangent addition identity gives (10)'s action.
None of \(\alpha+j\theta\) is \(\pi/2\) modulo \(\pi\):
such an equality would give
\[
1+4j=2m+4km
\]
for an integer \(k\), an impossibility because the left side is odd
and the right side even. This also applies to \(j=m\), so the map's
denominator is nonzero at every displayed orbit point. Distinct indices
between zero and \(m-1\) have distinct angles modulo \(\pi\), giving
distinct \(u_j\). Addition of \(m\theta=\pi\) returns to the start.

The fractional linear map has inverse given by replacing \(\theta\)
by \(-\theta\); its denominator has a real pole at \(u=\cot\theta\).
It is therefore outside the polynomial automorphism and fiber-affine
classes. Formula (1) proves output preservation and the cycle (12).

The second coordinate \((-u^3-u)/2\) is strictly decreasing in real
\(u\). Its \(m\) values are distinct, giving \(N_m\ge m\); at most
two scalars per pair gives \(N_m\le2m\). All pairs have output zero,
and their differences are distinct, proving (13). Apply the exact
completion inequality to obtain (14). \(\square\)

### Why it matters

The finite-orbit conclusion of NC-A2 cannot be extended to all rational
pair automorphisms. Nevertheless this explicit long-orbit construction
has controlled-pair exponent one in the actual scalar count, leaving
a quadratic complement. It fails the near-complete requirement for
a proved reason, without a carrier search.

### Scope

This is an exact boundary example, not a new asymptotic candidate.
Trigonometric constants are explicit real coefficients, and no
number-field or arithmetic amplification is used. Combining many cycles
while reusing their scalar coordinates could change the counts, but
would need a new proof and is not inferred from this example.

## 5. NC-A5 — polynomial output relabeling does not enlarge finite orbit certificates

### Meaning

Allowing a polynomial automorphism to change the output through a
one-variable polynomial still cannot merge output values. Invertibility
forces that output rule to be affine. On a finite invariant set, even a
growing library of these broader maps has at most four points per orbit.

### Exact statement — PROVED

Suppose \(\Phi\) is a polynomial automorphism and
\[
P\circ\Phi=\psi\circ P
\tag{15}
\]
for some real polynomial \(\psi\). Necessarily
\[
\boxed{H\circ\Phi\circ H^{-1}(u,z)
       =(a u+f(z),\alpha z+\beta),\qquad a\alpha\ne0,}
\tag{16}
\]
where \(a,\alpha,\beta\) are real constants and \(f\) is a polynomial.
Conversely every map in (16) is such an automorphism.

If any family of these maps preserves a common finite
\(E\subseteq A^2\), every generated orbit \(\mathcal O\) has at most
four points and at most two distinct outputs. More precisely,
\[
|\mathcal O|\le2|P(\mathcal O)|.
\tag{17}
\]
The certificate that counts each orbit's own output set and then the
uncontrolled complement therefore satisfies
\[
\boxed{\sum_{\mathcal O}|P(\mathcal O)|+N^2-Q
       \ge N^2-\lfloor Q/2\rfloor\ge\lceil N^2/2\rceil.}
\tag{18}
\]

### Proof

In fiber coordinates the map is \((F(u,z),\psi(z))\). A polynomial
automorphism has nonzero constant Jacobian determinant: the chain rule
with its polynomial inverse writes a product of two polynomials as 1,
so both are polynomial units. Consequently
\[
F_u(u,z)\psi'(z)
\]
is a nonzero constant. Each factor is therefore a nonzero constant.
Integrating gives \(F=a u+f(z)\) and \(\psi(z)=\alpha z+\beta\),
with \(a\alpha\ne0\). The converse follows from the explicit triangular
inverse.

Let \(Z=P(E)\). Each map permutes \(E\) and induces an affine
permutation of the finite ordered set \(Z\). By the finite-order
argument in NC-A2, this induced permutation is either identity or
the same order reversal of \(Z\). Thus the output orbit of any
point has at most two values.

The map also gives an affine bijection between the finite difference
sections \(E_z\) and \(E_{\alpha z+\beta}\). These sections have the
same size \(k\). It sends the \(i\)-th ordered difference to either
the \(i\)-th or the \((k+1-i)\)-th ordered difference of the target.
Every composition has the same rank property. There are at most two
reachable outputs, and at most two reachable ranks in each. This proves
the four-point bound and (17). Sum (17) over the disjoint orbits and
charge \(N^2-Q\) to obtain (18). \(\square\)

### Why it matters

This treats nonlinear polynomial pair automorphisms together with every
polynomial output rule, not just exact preservation. The forced output
map \(\alpha z+\beta\) is injective, so there is no hidden output
merging in (15). The conclusion is uniform in the number and degrees of
the rules.

### Scope

Different orbits can still share outputs; (18) is not an actual-image
lower bound. Maps without polynomial inverses, nonpolynomial output
rules, partial returns, and nonrational algebraic correspondences are
outside this theorem.
